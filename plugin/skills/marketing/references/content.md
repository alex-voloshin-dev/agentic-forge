# Marketing content (marketing sub-procedure)

Produce on-brand content — landing copy, social posts, or ad/paid copy — to the positioning and
proof points. Content is delivered as files (no typed handoff).

## Steps

1. **Read the positioning/strategy** (or the brief provided) — the positioning, the proof points,
   the audience, and the tone. If the repo keeps durable marketing state (see below), read
   `marketing/MARKETING.md` and `marketing/content-calendar.md` first.
2. **Pick the format** (landing headline + supporting lines / social post / ad copy) and its
   length/shape conventions (platform limits below for social).
3. **Write to the positioning.** Lead with the single most interesting concrete finding — not a
   topic introduction. Use only the **real proof points** — never invent benchmarks, percentages,
   or claims the brief doesn't support.
4. **Match the tone** stated in the brief.
5. **Run the anti-AI writing gate** (below) on every draft.
6. **Self-check** before finishing: every claim traces to a proof point; no unsupported
   superlatives; tone, format, and platform limits fit.

## Social: platform rules that move reach

- **Zero-click first.** Platforms suppress external links: deliver the full value *inside* the
  post; mention a domain as plain text; if a link is essential, put it in the first reply / "link
  in profile". The post must work for a reader who never clicks.
- **Personal beats company.** Recommend publishing from a personal account with the company named
  in the body — personal profiles measurably outperform company pages.
- **Limits (cut content, never quality):** X — 280 chars per tweet/reply; LinkedIn — the
  1,300–1,900-char band (below reads thin, above loses dwell); short-form platforms — under
  ~1,000 chars. Trim the middle (method/explanation), never the hook or the CTA.
- **End with a specific engagement question**, not a generic one.

## The anti-AI writing gate

Generated marketing copy has recognizable tells; strip them before delivery:

- No promotional vocabulary: "groundbreaking", "game-changing", "revolutionary", "best-in-class",
  "leverage", "delve", "crucial", "landscape", "pivotal", "showcase", "testament", "vibrant",
  "tapestry", "foster", "elevate".
- No forced rule-of-three ("innovation, inspiration, and insights") — use two, or four.
- No negative parallelisms ("It's not just X, it's Y"; "Not only X, but also Y").
- No tacked-on "-ing" depth ("…, highlighting the importance of…", "…, underscoring…").
- Plain copulas: "is/has", not "serves as / stands as / marks a".
- No generic closes ("the future looks bright", "exciting times ahead") and no filler ("in order
  to", "it is important to note").
- Do use: varied sentence lengths; specific numbers and names; a stated opinion ("this surprised
  us"); acknowledged uncertainty where honest; first person where it fits.

## Durable marketing state (optional, recommended for recurring work)

For repos with ongoing marketing, keep two committed files so strategy and publication state
survive between sessions: `marketing/MARKETING.md` (positioning, ICP, channels, voice — the
standing strategy this reference's briefs come from) and `marketing/content-calendar.md` (what
was published where, what's queued). Posts land as `marketing/posts/YYYY-MM-DD-<platform>-<slug>.md`
— one file per platform, no extra index/report files; update the calendar after creating posts.

## Rubric (gradeable)

- Reflects the positioning and audience; uses only supported proof points.
- No invented numbers/benchmarks; no unsupported superlatives; the anti-AI gate passes.
- Tone, format, and platform limits match; social posts deliver value zero-click.
